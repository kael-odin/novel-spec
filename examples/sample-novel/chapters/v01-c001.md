The sea gave up its offering at dawn.

Aren saw it from the lantern room — a dark shape tangled in the kelp line, too
long to be timber, too still to be a seal. He climbed down the spiral stair with
the brass key cold in his fist and the tide chart folded in his coat, because a
keeper checks everything twice and believes nothing the first time.

It was a woman. She was breathing, barely, and her fingers were locked around a
folded paper sealed in waxed cloth. He did not try to take it. A thing clutched
that tight is a thing a person has chosen to die for, and he was not yet ready
to test whether she would.

He dragged her above the tide line. His father's watch, in his pocket, bumped
against his hip with each step. It had stopped at seven minutes past three,
seven years ago, and he carried it the way a man carries a scar — not because it
still hurt, but because he had forgotten how to walk without it.

By the time he had her in the keeper's quarters, wrapped in wool, the rain had
turned to sleet. He fed the stove and watched her. The paper stayed in her hand.
Whatever it said, it could wait. The living come before the dead, and the note
was not going anywhere.

He did not yet know it was his daughter's hand.
